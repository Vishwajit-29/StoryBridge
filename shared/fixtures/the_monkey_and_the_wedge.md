# The Monkey and the Wedge
*From The Panchatantra (Public Domain)*

## Chapter 1: The Building Site
In the outskirts of a flourishing ancient city, a wealthy merchant began constructing a magnificent temple. Many artisans, carpenters, masons, and woodcutters were employed on the work site. Every noon, when the scorching sun reached its peak, all the workers would lay down their tools and head to the nearby town for their midday meal.

One day, a huge log of seasoned timber lay on the ground. A master carpenter had been sawing it down the center. Before departing for lunch, the carpenter split the log partially and hammered a sturdy wooden wedge firmly into the cleft so that the two separated halves of the log would not close up and pinch his saw.

## Chapter 2: The Curious Intruder
Near the temple grove lived a band of wild monkeys. As soon as the carpenters departed, the monkeys descended from the trees and began roaming through the deserted construction site. They leaped across scaffolding, played with wooden shavings, and inspected the unfamiliar objects left behind by the men.

Among them was a young, restless monkey who was overly inquisitive and could not resist meddling with things he did not understand. He noticed the huge log and the wedge sticking out right in the center of the deep fissure.

## Chapter 3: The Fatal Mischief
"What could this strange piece of wood be doing here?" the curious monkey thought. Without understanding the great tension stored between the split halves of the log, he sat astride the fissure, directly over the opening. With all his might, he began tugging, pulling, and wriggling the wedge back and forth.

Slowly, the wedge loosened from the tight grip of the wood. With one final, violent pull, the monkey yanked the wedge completely out of the cleft. Instantly, the massive halves of the log snapped shut with tremendous force, trapping the foolish monkey.

## Chapter 4: The Moral
When the carpenters returned from their lunch, they found the monkey caught in the log. The wise elder among the artisans remarked to his companions: "One should never meddle in affairs that do not concern them, nor tamper with forces they do not comprehend."
